# Transom, motor well and framing — evidence and model decisions

## Factory construction

The [1974 construction article](https://www.barque.ru/projects/1974/powerboat_trimaran_sarepta),
particularly its exploded sections and construction nodes, distinguishes the
transom panel, motor-well assembly, bottom section and side sections.
The panel meets the hull bottom; its lower outline follows the V bottom
section. A separate raised floor belongs to the motor well (recess). It is
not the outside hull bottom, nor a flat insert replacing the V bottom.

The article explicitly describes three knees connecting the transom to
longitudinal bottom framing, motor-well side walls connecting it to the sides,
and an oak motor board reinforced by angles. Most hull joints are welded
(spot and argon-arc), with riveting in inaccessible places. This is an
aluminium-magnesium AMg5 hull, not a soft-soldered assembly.
Water leaves the motor well through scuppers. Exact well-floor height,
slope, scupper layout, panel rake and notch width were not found.

Longitudinal side/deck corrugations are structural stiffening features.
The published bottom/side flange is 30 mm wide and serves as a spray rail.
It is distinct from the protective rim at the upper side/deck joint.
The construction drawing shows transverse floors, longitudinal bottom
stiffeners and side frames. The supplied kit photos support this arrangement,
but their central X lattice is not adopted as measured factory framing.

The [1980 changes](https://www.barque.ru/advice/1980/modifications_motor_boat_sarepta)
describe later Z-profile stringers and Pi-section floors above them.
This reconstruction uses an early T-profile interpretation rather than
claiming a complete late-production interior.

## Revised model

- Selected envelope: early article 4600 x 1600; side/deck datum 750.
  These do not resolve the separate passport length/beam ambiguity.
- Centre engine seat: passport 375 above keel datum. Notch shoulders: 425;
  notch depth 50 and width 360 are photo estimates, not published dimensions.
- Motor well: forward bulkhead at X=4000; floor Z=230 forward to Z=215 aft.
  This is an explicit drainage-oriented reconstruction, not a measured angle.
  The transom panel is vertical in this version; its actual rake is unresolved.
- Cockpit opening: published 2600 x 1280; estimated X=1400..4000.
- Closed bow locker separated from cockpit by a complete transverse bulkhead.
- Five transverse floor/side frames at estimated X=1700, 2200, 2700, 3200,
  3700; three longitudinal bottom members and three hidden transom knees.
  Frame count/spacing, floor level and profile simplifications are reconstructed.
- Nominal model walls 1.2, transom 2.0; framing web >=0.65; chine flange
  thickness >=0.35; side ridge projection >=0.16, all in model millimetres.
  These print allowances deliberately exceed literal metal thicknesses.
- Upper seam Z=670 at bow to 550 aft; top deck Z=750 throughout.
  Upper setback 40..50 gives a plausible inward slope and room for the cockpit.
  Neither seam heights nor setbacks are factory measurements.
- The lower guide profiles were re-faired only towards their upper ends;
  aft endpoint heights, 1300 chine beam and approximately 8.5-degree deadrise
  remain. The overall plan and continuous rim retain the accepted photo target.

Scuppers, oak-board texture, individual rivets and accurate profile sections
are not included. Further photos or survey data are needed for those details.
The cabin, hatch lid and outboard motors remain separate future parts.

## Subsequent side-photo correction

The user's real-boat side photographs show horizontal corrugations ending
before the upturned bow and continuing to the transom. Fixed side-surface
fractions were replaced with constant-height intersections at Z=280 and 425,
X=1600..4600, with flat forward and flush aft ends. These coordinates remain
photo-derived estimates. The separate chine flange retains its curved bow path.

## Subsequent interior revision

The stepped bow boundary, four backed pockets and widened cabin landings
are documented in `INTERIOR_RESEARCH.md`. The original frame definition is
unchanged. The landings now cover the frame tips. Two estimated scuppers are
present; earlier statements that scuppers were omitted describe the previous
milestone. This does not establish their exact factory layout.

## Subsequent bow plan revision

The well wall remains at X=4000 while the upper cockpit front moves to
X=1600. A 200-forward footwell now places its lower wall at X=1400. This
keeps a 2600 interior reference including the leg area, with a 2400 upper
opening. The datum interpretation is reconstructed; see `BOW_PLAN_REVIEW.md`.
Lower guide and framing definitions are retained.
